# Clinical sources and scene map

**Why the Heart Team may choose coronary bypass surgery**

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 01

Sources checked on **13 November 2026**. Current US specialty guidance governs the distinctions between chronic disease and acute coronary syndromes. NHLBI and NHS patient materials guide plain-language explanations of the operation and recovery. Anatomy references establish where the graft receives and delivers blood. The clinical decision and procedure shown are illustrative and individual care varies by person and hospital.

## References

- **A · ACC/AHA/SCAI coronary revascularization guideline, 2021.** Published online 9 December 2021 and in the 18 January 2022 issue. The stable-disease recommendations distinguish significant left main disease, reduced left-ventricular function, and diabetes with multivessel disease involving the LAD. The Heart Team table covers anatomy, health risks, technical factors and patient preferences. The guideline describes an internal mammary graft to the LAD. [AHA guideline summary](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2021-acc-aha-scai-guideline-for-coronary-artery-revascularization/top-things-to-know) · [Guideline executive summary and recommendations](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001039?download=true)
- **B · AHA/ACC and partner societies, chronic coronary disease guideline, 2023.** Published online 20 July 2023. The treatment and revascularization sections support ongoing medical therapy, evaluation of symptom burden, selected procedures for persistent lifestyle-limiting angina with treatable significant disease, and survival benefit in specific groups. [Full guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001168)
- **C · ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI acute coronary syndromes guideline, 2025.** Published online 27 February 2025 and in the 1 April 2025 issue. Section 5.2 covers primary PCI and urgent CABG in STEMI. Section 5.2.2 limits urgent surgery after infeasible or unsuccessful PCI to selected patients with substantial heart muscle at risk and suitable targets. Section 7.4 considers other disease and treatment timing after initial reperfusion. [Full guideline and section 5.2.2](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309?download=true) · [Official AHA summary](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2025-guideline-for-the-management-of-patients-with-acute-coronary-syndromes/top-things-to-know)
- **D · NHLBI patient information on indications, tests and preparation.** “Who Needs It?” was updated 28 August 2024. The other pages explain the different roles of angiography and echocardiography and the individual instructions before surgery. [Who Needs It?](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/coronary-artery-bypass-grafting/who-needs) · [Angina diagnosis and tests](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/angina/diagnosis) · [Preparing for Surgery](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/coronary-artery-bypass-grafting/preparing)
- **E · NHLBI and NHS patient information on the operation.** NHLBI updated its surgery page on 24 March 2022. NHS reviewed its procedure page on 3 July 2025. These sources explain healthy vessels used as grafts, general anaesthesia, the conventional on-pump approach, selected alternatives and immediate recovery. [NHLBI · What to Expect During Surgery](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/coronary-artery-bypass-grafting/during) · [NHS · How a CABG is done](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-artery-bypass-graft/how-its-done/)
- **F · Graft conduit anatomy.** The Society of Thoracic Surgeons’ 2016 guideline supports the use of an internal thoracic artery to bypass the LAD when indicated. A published anatomical study verifies the usual subclavian origin. Two treating-hospital patient booklets document retaining the native chest-artery connection in the in-place example and attaching a free graft to the aorta and a downstream coronary target in the common free-graft example. [STS arterial-conduit guideline](https://www.sts.org/sites/default/files/documents/Clinical%20Guidelines/Guidelines_ArterialConduitsforCABG.pdf) · [Internal thoracic artery anatomical study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4583587/) · [Sir Charles Gairdner Hospital cardiac surgery booklet](https://www.scgh.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/HSPs/NMHS/Hospitals/SCGH/Documents/Patients/Cardiac-surgery---Patient-information.pdf?thn=0) · [Liverpool Heart and Chest Hospital booklet](https://www.lhch.nhs.uk/media/.leaflets/64ad25be7db1a6.66828677.pdf)
- **G · AHA scientific statement on prevention after CABG, 2026 update.** Published online 13 May 2026 and in the 23 June 2026 issue. Supports continued coronary risk management, prescribed therapy, structured follow-up, coordinated discharge and rehabilitation. It is a scientific statement on care after CABG. [AHA scientific statement and references](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/secondary-prevention-after-coronary-artery-bypass-graft-surgery-2026-update) · [Primary publication, PMID 42125794](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42125794/)
- **H · NHLBI and NHS patient recovery and complication information.** NHLBI updated its recovery page 24 March 2022. NHS reviewed its recovery and complications pages 3 July 2025. These sources support monitoring, temporary breathing assistance or drains where appropriate, gradual activity, wound guidance, follow-up and different levels of urgency for wound changes versus possible heart attack or stroke. [NHLBI · Recovery](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/coronary-artery-bypass-grafting/recovery) · [NHS · Recovery](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-artery-bypass-graft/recovery/) · [NHS · Complications and when to seek help](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-artery-bypass-graft/complications/)
