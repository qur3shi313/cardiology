## Graft continuity

- **In-place chest artery example.** Use an anterior heart and thoracic aorta view, with the patient's left clearly oriented. Keep the left subclavian connection intact throughout. Draw the distal graft joining the side of the LAD below the lesion. The visible flow sequence is left subclavian → left internal thoracic graft → downstream LAD and its branches.
- **Free vein graft example.** Show a distinct vessel segment as the graft, with the example proximal junction on the ascending aorta and the distal junction beyond the lesion. The visible flow sequence is aorta → vein graft → downstream coronary branches. A vein used as a graft carries oxygen-rich arterial blood in this circuit. Use a warm bright blood marker inside both graft types. A teal wall can identify the graft without implying low oxygen.
- The native coronary narrowing stays unchanged. Keep the original vessel centerline, plaque, and supplied territory anchored. Let the audience see the graft join before particles cross it. Flow direction and apparent speed are schematic. Avoid showing a generic vein graft and a chest artery sharing the same upstream connection.
- Show one educational depiction of an attached graft without incision, instrument-handling, stitch placement, cannulation, numeric parameters, or an operative checklist. Keep the procedure explanation in narration and reveal each anatomical state separately.
