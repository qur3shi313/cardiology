# Clinical source map

**How a bypass graft routes blood around a blockage**

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 02

Sources checked on **13 November 2026**. Episode 01 explains why bypass may be selected. This episode explains graft inflow, the connection beyond a lesion, and coronary outflow. The cases are original educational illustrations of two common configurations.

## References

- **A · NHLBI, How Blood Flows through the Heart, updated 25 June 2025.** The coronary circulation section explains that the coronary arteries branch from the aorta, supply oxygen and nutrients to the myocardium, and that the LAD and circumflex are left coronary branches. An original 1992 coronary angiographic anatomy study maps marginal branches of the circumflex to the lateral left-ventricular wall. [NHLBI heart blood flow](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart/blood-flow) · [Dodge et al., Circulation](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.cir.86.1.232)
- **B · NHLBI, What to Expect During CABG, and NHS, How a CABG is done.** NHLBI's page was updated 24 March 2022. The NHS text was reviewed 3 July 2025. Both explain that surgeons use a blood vessel from elsewhere in the body and attach it so blood can flow beyond the diseased segment. The embedded third-party video and illustrations are outside the production assets. [NHLBI CABG procedure](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/coronary-artery-bypass-grafting/during) · [NHS CABG procedure](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-artery-bypass-graft/how-its-done/)
- **C · Society of Thoracic Surgeons, Adult Cardiac Surgery Database training manual, version 4.20.2, May 2026.** The CABG conduit examples identify an in-situ LIMA to LAD and an aortic saphenous-vein graft to OM1. The CAB distal position definition and examples near item 2730 describe the end-to-side configuration. This is a technical anatomy reference rather than a treatment recommendation. [STS data specifications and example diagrams](https://www.sts.org/sites/default/files/Database%20Manuals/Training%20Manual%20V4_20_2%20May%202026%20Volume%201.pdf)
- **D · Surgical anatomy of the internal thoracic arteries, 2015, and STS arterial conduit guidelines, 2016.** The original cadaveric study documents the usual subclavian origin and the course of the internal thoracic arteries behind the costal cartilages. The STS guidance identifies the internal thoracic artery as a conduit to the LAD when that artery is selected for bypass and discusses alternative conduits. The February 2026 STS database anatomy education shows the usual left subclavian as the last of three arch branches and explains its supply to the left arm. [Original anatomy study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4583587/) · [STS arterial conduit guideline](https://www.sts.org/sites/default/files/documents/Clinical%20Guidelines/Guidelines_ArterialConduitsforCABG.pdf) · [STS anatomy education](https://www.sts.org/sites/default/files/Database%20Manuals/ACSD%20Monthly%20Webinar%2002042026.pdf)
- **E · ACC/AHA/SCAI coronary revascularization guideline, 2021.** Its bypass-conduit section discusses selection of the conduit and includes use of the radial artery when appropriate for a significantly stenosed non-LAD target. The episode uses a free vein graft as an example and makes no claim about which conduit an individual should receive. [Executive summary and conduit recommendation](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001039?download=true)
- **F · Gaudino et al., Circulation, 2021, transit-time flow measurement.** The systematic review and expert opinion describe intraoperative graft-flow assessment, use of a perivascular ultrasound probe, and interpretation in context. They discuss competitive native flow, graft geometry and target-vessel characteristics. The STS conduit guideline also discusses the effect of native flow in selected settings. [Circulation full report](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.054311) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34606302/)
- **G · AHA scientific statement on secondary prevention after CABG, 2026.** Published online 13 May 2026. Covers long-term graft patency, disease progression, clinician-led prevention, structured follow-up and rehabilitation. [AHA statement and references](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/secondary-prevention-after-coronary-artery-bypass-graft-surgery-2026-update) · [Primary publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42125794/)
- **H · Liverpool Heart and Chest Hospital CABG patient booklet and NHLBI CABG recovery.** The hospital booklet's CABG page illustrates a chest-artery graft and a vein graft in the same teaching diagram. NHLBI recovery guidance supports the brief handoff to monitoring, medicines, rehabilitation and the next episode. [Liverpool Heart and Chest Hospital, CABG patient information](https://www.lhch.nhs.uk/media/.leaflets/64ad25be7db1a6.66828677.pdf) · [NHLBI CABG recovery](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/coronary-artery-bypass-grafting/recovery)
