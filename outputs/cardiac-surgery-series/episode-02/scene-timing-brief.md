## Anatomy, motion and color

- **Case A.** From the aortic arch, keep the left subclavian origin of the internal thoracic artery visible. The left internal thoracic artery stays connected proximally, with its distal end joined to the LAD beyond the lesion. The normal artery layout in P02, the matched junction in P03 and the flow route in P04 share the same LAD plaque shape and distal branch landmark.
- **Case B.** The ascending aorta supplies a free segment of great saphenous vein. It enters the side of the named obtuse marginal branch after that branch's narrowing. Place the marginal branch on the left lateral heart surface and distinguish its parent circumflex from the LAD. The graft tube does not overlap a coronary crossing in a way that reads as a third connection.
- **Color and direction.** Keep flowing blood markers the same warm color in both grafts and in the native coronary circulation. A cool green graft wall distinguishes the conduit. Each tracer follows a continuous inside-lumen path that starts in the named source. Markers do not represent velocity, flow measurement, pressure or oxygen saturation.
- **Motion.** Use eased camera changes between anatomically matched views. Lock the frame while a tracer crosses a junction. Advance labels with narration and show new detail at meaningful cues. Check all parts of each graft at a phone-size view, especially the open distal join.
