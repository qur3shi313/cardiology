# Clinical sources and scene map

**Intensive care and recovery after coronary bypass surgery**

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 03

Clinical sources checked on **13 November 2026**. This patient-facing episode follows an illustrated recovery after CABG. Published hospital examples describe how their own teams work. The animation presents the purpose of equipment and decisions and uses individualised timing and discharge instructions.

## Dated references

- **A · NHLBI, Recovery from Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting, updated 24 March 2022.** Covers hospital monitoring, drains, urine collection, oxygen, rhythm, pain relief, possible temporary pacing, changes in sleep and appetite, follow-up, and supervised cardiac rehabilitation. [NHLBI CABG recovery](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/coronary-artery-bypass-grafting/recovery)
- **B · NHS, How a CABG is done, and Recovering from a CABG, text reviewed 3 July 2025.** The procedure page supports initial ventilation, transition to supplemental oxygen where needed, drains, pain relief and transfer to intensive care. The recovery page covers ward transfer, staff-assisted movement, readiness for discharge, medicines, wound care, help at home, and rehabilitation. The embedded procedure video has a separate media review date. [NHS CABG procedure](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-artery-bypass-graft/how-its-done/) · [NHS CABG recovery](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-artery-bypass-graft/recovery/)
- **C · Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust, Your Journey Through Heart Surgery, reviewed 27 August 2026.** Patient information on waking with a breathing tube, inability to speak through it, nonverbal communication with nurses, pain, assisted sitting and walking, home activity, and rehabilitation. Published timing and service details describe the trust's own pathway. [Leeds heart surgery patient guide](https://www.leedsth.nhs.uk/patients/resources/your-journey-through-heart-surgery-2/)
- **D · Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust, Heart surgery and what to expect, published December 2023, review due December 2026.** Patient guide explains the cardiac monitor, oxygen sensor, arterial pressure monitoring, IV access, chest drainage, bladder catheter and temporary pacing wires. Its dates for removing supports and dressing changes describe local practice. [Guy's and St Thomas' heart surgery guide](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/heart-surgery-and-what-expect)
- **E · Grant et al., joint ERAS Cardiac Society, ERAS International Society and STS perioperative consensus, published online 28 January 2024.** Postoperative sections cover early extubation in selected settings, individualised mobility and sternal guidance, delirium screening and cardiac rehabilitation. The separate Cambridge University Hospitals guide supports non-clinical family participation such as reporting changes, offering orientation and asking about glasses or hearing aids. [Joint cardiac surgery consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38284956/) · [Consensus full report](https://www.annalsthoracicsurgery.org/article/S0003497523013176/pdf) · [Cambridge University Hospitals critical-care delirium information, accessed 13 November 2026](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/delirium-in-adult-critical-care-general-information/)
- **F · Ruel et al., AHA scientific statement on secondary prevention after CABG, published online 13 May 2026.** Supports clinician-led long-term prevention, coordinated discharge and follow-up, and cardiac rehabilitation. The episode discusses following prescribed care and asking about local access, without specific medicines or doses. [AHA 2026 scientific statement](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/secondary-prevention-after-coronary-artery-bypass-graft-surgery-2026-update) · [Primary publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42125794/)
- **G · NHS emergency guidance and CDC stroke signs.** NHS CABG Complications, reviewed 3 July 2025, distinguishes prompt contact for worsening wound signs or fever from immediate emergency action for possible heart attack or stroke. NHS Shortness of Breath, accessed 13 November 2026, identifies severe breathing difficulty as an emergency. CDC stroke information dated 19 May 2026 calls for emergency help for any of the signs. The episode gives face, arm and speech examples and uses the local emergency number for an international audience. [NHS CABG complications and urgency](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-artery-bypass-graft/complications/) · [NHS severe breathing difficulty and urgency](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/shortness-of-breath/) · [CDC stroke signs and urgent action](https://www.cdc.gov/stroke/signs-symptoms/index.html)
