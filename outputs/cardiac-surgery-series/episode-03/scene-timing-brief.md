## Device continuity and clinical design

- **Ventilation and oxygen.** The ventilator is attached to the secured tracheal tube. Keep the line connected and in one consistent position while in use. The later simple oxygen mask or nasal cannula is a separate support after the tube is gone. The clinical team makes the transition off camera.
- **Monitoring and treatment.** Trace ECG leads to the ECG monitor, the finger probe to the oxygen display, and the arterial line to its pressure trace. Keep the IV line separate from the arterial line. Illustrate the external paths without exposing insertion points or showing numerical targets, drug names, or simulated alarms.
- **Collection and pacing.** The chest drain leaves the covered chest and leads to its own upright collection unit. The urinary catheter path emerges from beneath the blanket and leads to a separate urine measuring bag, with no visible groin or catheter entry point. Temporary pacing is an explicitly optional state with a separate external device. No access or removal technique is illustrated.
- **Progression.** Use steady framing while a device’s function is explained. Use a new view as a support changes, the patient moves or the written plan becomes relevant. The same character can use different clothing in the ward and at home while retaining a recognisable cap, colour scheme and profile.
- **Safety messages.** At 390-pixel phone width, the care-team instruction and emergency instruction must be fully readable. Hold the emergency instruction continuously during its three symptom examples, with warm maroon plus high-contrast text. Avoid assigning an emergency number to an international audience.
