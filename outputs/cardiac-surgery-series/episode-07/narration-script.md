# Surgical tricuspid valve repair

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 07

English narration for ElevenLabs Alexander. Say tricuspid as try-KUSS-pid and annuloplasty as AN-yoo-loh-plas-tee. Record only the nine spoken paragraphs.

## P01 · A leak on the right side of the heart

On the right side of the heart, the tricuspid valve lets blood pass from the right atrium to the right ventricle. It should close when the ventricle pumps blood toward the lungs. When it does not close well, some blood goes back into the atrium. We’ll see how a support ring can help the valve close.

## P02 · Why a valve with intact leaflets can leak

Here, the leaflets are still there, but the rim around them has stretched. Their edges can no longer meet in the middle during the squeeze. The leak can contribute to a build-up of fluid, sometimes causing swollen legs or an enlarged belly. A heart ultrasound helps show how much the valve leaks and how the right heart is responding.

## P03 · Why repair is chosen in this case

The team considers the amount of leakage, the valve’s shape, how well the right ventricle pumps and what other surgery is planned. This patient is already having surgery on a diseased mitral valve. The team decides to repair the tricuspid valve in the same operation. That decision is made case by case, since adding a procedure brings its own risks.

## P04 · Reaching the tricuspid valve

The person is asleep under general anaesthesia. A heart-lung machine supports circulation during this operation. The surgeon reaches the tricuspid valve from the right atrium. We’ll stay with this valve in the animation. From the atrial side, you can see the original leaflets surrounded by the widened rim.

## P05 · Supporting the rim with a ring

The surgeon secures a support ring around the valve opening. This is called annuloplasty. It brings the stretched rim closer to a shape that lets the leaflets meet. The ring does not open and close. The original leaflets still do that work. Watch the gap between their edges get smaller as the rim changes shape.

## P06 · Checking the repair under flow

As the heart resumes its work, the team uses ultrasound to check the valve. In this example, the leaflets meet more closely when the ventricle squeezes, and there is less backward flow. The valve still opens for filling. The team checks for a remaining leak, narrowing and how the right ventricle is working.

## P07 · Monitoring and recovery

Recovery includes close checks on circulation and heart rhythm. Some people need a permanent pacemaker if the heart’s electrical signals do not travel normally after surgery. The team will explain what it is watching for. Before going home, ask about wound care, medicines, activity, cardiac rehabilitation and the next valve check.

## P08 · Risks and when to call

Bleeding, infection, rhythm problems and a leak that persists or returns are possible. Follow the contact plan for fever or changes around the wound. If you have severe difficulty breathing or sudden signs of stroke, get emergency help. Regular review remains important because the right heart can change even after the valve has been repaired.

## P09 · What the ring changes

This ring supports the valve’s rim so the original leaflets can meet more effectively. It did not replace the valve. Next we move a short distance along the right ventricle’s outflow to the pulmonary valve, and follow an adult who needs a replacement after childhood heart surgery.
