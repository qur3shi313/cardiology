# Valve-Sparing Aortic Root Replacement: The David Operation

## P01 · A problem with the wall

An enlarged aortic root can make the entrance to the aorta wider than it should be. The root includes the place where the aortic valve sits and where the coronary arteries begin. The wall can be diseased even when the valve leaflets themselves are still suitable for repair or preservation.

## P02 · Why treatment is considered

An enlarged root may cause the valve to leak if the leaflets cannot meet properly. A growing aneurysm also raises concern about a tear or rupture. The team uses scans to measure the aorta and ultrasound to examine the valve. Decisions also account for growth, family history and underlying conditions.

## P03 · Who may keep the valve?

The David operation is one form of valve-sparing root replacement. It can be an option when surgery is needed and the native valve can be preserved. The surgeon assesses its shape and tissue quality; if preservation is unsuitable, a replacement valve may be needed instead. That choice is specific to the patient.

## P04 · Keep the native leaflets

In this example, the heart-lung machine supports circulation while the surgical team works on the root. The enlarged wall is removed. The native leaflets and their supporting attachments are kept, and each coronary opening is preserved with a small rim of surrounding tissue. The arteries themselves still lead to the same heart muscle.

## P05 · Support the valve in a graft

A fabric tube replaces the enlarged root. In reimplantation, the native valve sits inside this graft and its support points are secured to the new wall. This stabilizes the valve's surroundings so its leaflets can meet when it closes. The moving valve in this example is still the patient's own.

## P06 · Reconnect the coronary arteries

The coronary arteries need their supply from the new root. Each of the two preserved openings is reconnected separately to the graft above the valve. The other end of the graft joins the remaining aorta. This re-creates the route to the body and the two routes that supply the heart itself.

## P07 · Check how it works

When the heart resumes pumping, the team uses ultrasound to check whether the native valve opens and closes well and whether it still leaks. In the animation, the same leaflets open for forward flow and close between beats. A good-looking graft does not, by itself, show that the valve works normally.

## P08 · What recovery still requires

Recovery includes close observation for complications such as bleeding, rhythm problems or stroke. A preserved valve can still develop a leak over time. Follow-up checks both valve function and the rest of the aorta, with the timing and type of imaging tailored to the person and the underlying condition.

## P09 · The next choice

The key difference is what gets replaced: the David operation puts in a new root but keeps a suitable native aortic valve. In the next episode, the Bentall operation replaces both the root and the valve together. The right option depends on which tissues can safely be kept.
