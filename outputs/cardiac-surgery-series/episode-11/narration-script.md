# Bentall Operation: Replacing the Aortic Root and Valve

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 11

English narration for ElevenLabs Alexander. Say Ben-TALL or BEN-tall consistently; explain conduit as a tube with a valve inside.

## P01 · When the wall and valve need treatment

The aortic root is the first section of the aorta. Its wall surrounds the valve, and the coronary arteries begin just above it. If the root needs replacement and the valve cannot be preserved, the Bentall operation replaces both. This is a different operation from replacing the valve alone.

## P02 · Why these parts matter

An aneurysm can enlarge the root and increase the risk of a tear or rupture. A damaged valve may allow blood to leak back into the heart. Here the root is enlarged and the valve does not seal. The team uses scans and ultrasound to decide what can safely be preserved.

## P03 · One replacement with two parts

A valved conduit combines a replacement valve with a fabric tube. The tube replaces the root; the valve controls the direction of blood leaving the heart. This example uses a tissue valve. Mechanical valves are another option, and the team discusses how each choice affects follow-up and medicines.

## P04 · Remove the diseased root and valve

The heart-lung machine supports circulation while the team works. In this example the diseased root wall and the old valve are removed. The two coronary origins are saved with small rims of nearby tissue. That preserves the arteries for reconnection, because both still need a route to receive blood.

## P05 · Place the valved conduit

The new conduit sits where the diseased root and valve used to be. The replacement valve occupies the original valve position, and the tube extends upward as the new root. To restore circulation, the graft must also join the remaining aorta and connect separately to both coronary arteries.

## P06 · Reconnect both coronary arteries

Each preserved coronary opening is reattached to its own opening in the new graft, above the replacement valve. The rest of each artery stays in its original course. The new root can then send blood both onward into the aorta and into the arteries that supply the heart muscle.

## P07 · Check valve function and flow

As the heart takes over circulation again, the team checks how the replacement valve opens and closes and looks for leakage or other problems. Compare the new valve closing with the gap in the old valve at the start. The operation addresses the damaged root and valve; the remaining aorta still needs surveillance.

## P08 · Recovery and valve-specific care

After surgery, the team watches for complications such as bleeding, stroke or rhythm problems. The medicine plan depends in part on the type of valve. Mechanical valves generally require lifelong anticoagulation. Tissue valves can wear out over time. Either choice needs follow-up, including checks of the valve and the aorta.

## P09 · What the Bentall changes

The Bentall operation gives the heart a new aortic valve and a new root, with the coronary arteries connected back to their blood supply. In the next episode, we look at a different emergency: an acute type A aortic dissection, and why the location of the tear changes the operation.
