# Acute Type A Aortic Dissection: Ascending and Hemiarch Repair

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 12

English narration for ElevenLabs Alexander. Say AY-or-tik, HEM-ee-ark and false LOO-men. Calm delivery; the emergency call to action should remain clear.

## P01 · A split inside the aortic wall

In an aortic dissection, blood enters a split in the wall of the aorta. It can create a second channel beside the original one. A moving flap separates them. Type A means the ascending aorta, the section rising from the heart, is involved. This is a life-threatening emergency.

## P02 · Why an emergency team is needed

A dissection can rupture or reduce blood supply to vital organs. Near the heart, it can also interfere with the aortic valve or blood supply to the heart muscle. Sudden severe chest or back pain can be a warning sign. If that happens, get emergency help immediately. Symptoms are not identical for everyone.

## P03 · Use imaging to map the tear

When possible, a CT scan maps the dissection, where it begins, and what branches may be affected. The team also assesses the root and valve. In this example the entry tear is in the ascending aorta, the root and valve can be kept, and the arch itself has no separate entry tear.

## P04 · Support circulation during repair

The surgical team uses a heart-lung machine during open repair. For this example, the work extends to the nearby arch, so the team plans how to protect the brain and other organs while that part of the aorta is repaired. The method depends on the anatomy and the patient's condition.

## P05 · Replace the torn ascending section

The main tear in this example lies in the ascending aorta. The damaged segment around that tear is replaced with a fabric graft. The graft joins near the preserved root. It creates a new single passage there, while the native valve and the coronary arteries stay in their original positions.

## P06 · Extend to the adjoining arch

This example includes a hemiarch repair. The graft replaces the ascending aorta and part of the inner curve of the nearby arch. It does not replace the whole arch or move its branches. Other patterns of dissection can need a different repair, especially if the root or arch itself is badly affected.

## P07 · What the repair changes

After the connections are complete, blood can again flow through the repaired section toward the body. This does not mean every part of the dissection has disappeared. Farther down, part of the native aorta may still have two channels. The team checks circulation and looks for problems that still need attention.

## P08 · Recovery and lifelong imaging

Recovery includes close checks for bleeding, stroke and problems affecting the kidneys or other organs. After discharge, blood pressure management and repeat aortic imaging are important. A later scan may detect a change in a part of the aorta that was not replaced. The follow-up plan is specific to the patient.

## P09 · The lesson from this example

This repair replaces the most urgent damaged segment and preserves what can safely remain. The exact operation depends on the tear and the structures it affects.
