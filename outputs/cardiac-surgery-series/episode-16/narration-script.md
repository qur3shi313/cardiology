# How a donor heart is connected in a heart transplant

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 16

English narration for ElevenLabs Alexander. Say “bi-CAY-vul.” Record the paragraphs only.

## P01 · A different operation for advanced heart failure

A durable assist device helps a person’s own heart move blood. An orthotopic heart transplant replaces the failing heart with a donor heart in the usual position in the chest. This can be an option for some people with advanced heart failure. In our illustrated example, follow the connections that allow the donor heart to receive blood and pump it to the lungs and the body.

## P02 · Assessment and the donor offer

A transplant team assesses the heart condition, other organs, overall health, and the support needed for care after surgery. If transplantation is suitable, the team explains waiting and how donor offers are evaluated. Factors such as blood group, body size and the medical suitability of the organ matter. The clinical team reviews the individual offer before the operation goes ahead.

## P03 · The machine that supports circulation

The recipient is asleep under general anaesthesia. During the transplant, a heart-lung machine temporarily keeps oxygenated blood circulating while the surgical team replaces the heart. In this model the machine has a separate circulation path. The donor heart will take over pumping after the team has completed the connections and checked how it is working.

## P04 · Preparing the place for the donor heart

In this bicaval example, the failing heart is removed while the team leaves the connections needed for the new one. A rim of the recipient’s left atrium stays attached to the veins from the lungs. Two large veins from the body, the upper and lower vena cava, are kept as separate connections. The aorta and pulmonary artery also remain available to join.

## P05 · Joining the donor heart

The donor heart sits in the space where the old heart was. Its left atrium joins the retained left atrial rim. The surgeon also joins the two vena cava separately so blood from the body can enter the donor right atrium. The donor pulmonary artery connects to the route to the lungs, and the donor aorta to the route out to the body. Follow each connection on the model.

## P06 · Checking circulation through the new heart

Blood from the body enters through the two large veins and travels through the right side of the new heart to the lungs. Oxygen-rich blood returns through the lung veins to the left side and leaves through the aorta. The team checks pumping and circulation and gradually reduces support from the heart-lung machine when the new heart can take over.

## P07 · Recovery and early risks

In intensive care, staff watch how the donor heart pumps and check for bleeding, infection and other complications. Sometimes the transplanted heart needs extra support early after surgery. Over time, the immune system can attack the new heart. This is called rejection. The transplant team looks for signs of rejection and may detect it even when the patient feels well.

## P08 · Medicines and long-term follow-up

People who receive a heart transplant need long-term medicines that suppress the immune response. These medicines help prevent rejection and also make some infections more likely. Follow-up includes checks such as blood tests and heart imaging, and some programmes use heart tissue samples to look for rejection. Ask your own team how to take your medicines and whom to contact if you develop symptoms or cannot take a dose.

## P09 · The operation and the years after it

The operation restores a route for blood through the donor heart. Long-term care helps protect that heart and monitor the effects of treatment. Next, we move to congenital heart surgery and close an opening between the upper chambers.
