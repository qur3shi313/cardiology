# Clinical sources and scene map

**How a patch closes a ventricular septal defect**

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 18

Clinical sources checked on **13 November 2026**. The example is an isolated perimembranous ventricular septal defect repaired from the right atrium through the tricuspid valve.

## Dated references

- **A · American Heart Association, Ventricular Septal Defect, reviewed 3 August 2026.** Supports location, left-to-right flow, symptoms by defect size, patch materials, selection considerations and continuing follow-up. [AHA patient and anatomy guide](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/congenital-heart-defects/about-congenital-heart-defects/ventricular-septal-defect-vsd)
- **B · Zhao et al., Tricuspid valve detachment in closure of congenital ventricular septal defect, 2003.** The original surgical series documents transatrial exposure through the tricuspid valve, the relationship to surrounding structures and postoperative rhythm and valve assessment. Use the anatomic relationship to explain the ordinary view, with no depiction of leaflet detachment. [Original operative study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12638669/)
- **C · Johns Hopkins Medicine, Ventricular Septal Defect Surgery for Children, accessed 13 November 2026.** Supports surgical patch repair with temporary bypass, possible bleeding, infection, heart block and residual defect, and care during recovery. No page-level publication date was relied upon. [Johns Hopkins surgery guide](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/treatment-tests-and-therapies/ventricular-septal-defect-surgery-for-children)
- **D · American Heart Association, Tetralogy of Fallot, accessed 13 November 2026.** Supports the final transition to a distinct operation that closes the ventricular defect and improves the route to the lungs. [AHA tetralogy of Fallot guide](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/congenital-heart-defects/about-congenital-heart-defects/tetralogy-of-fallot)
