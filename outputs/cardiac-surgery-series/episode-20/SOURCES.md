# Clinical source map

**Arterial switch operation for d-transposition of the great arteries**

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 20

References checked 13 November 2026. The episode depicts a neonatal arterial switch with coronary transfer and an anterior position for the pulmonary bifurcation. Coronary courses vary, so the art depicts a simplified example.

## References

- **A · American Heart Association, d-Transposition of the Great Arteries, reviewed 15 April 2026.** Explains parallel circulation, early treatment and mixing, artery and coronary reconnection, and lifelong congenital follow-up. [AHA patient and family resource](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/congenital-heart-defects/about-congenital-heart-defects/d-transposition-of-the-great-arteries)
- **B · Children's Minnesota and The Children's Heart Clinic, Arterial Switch Operation, reviewed June 2023.** Describes temporary bypass, new aortic and pulmonary roots, coronary transfer, anterior pulmonary artery arrangement and early hospital care. The PDF is an anatomy and care reference for original art. [Institutional arterial switch guide](https://www.childrensmn.org/Departments/cardiovascular/heart-illustrations/surgery/english/aso.pdf)
- **C · Fricke and colleagues, Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery, published online 11 February 2021, print January 2022.** A retrospective series documents later reintervention and neoaortic valve or root changes. The episode uses no individual outcome or cohort percentages. [Original cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33715839/)
- **D · ACC/AHA/HRS/ISACHD/SCAI adult congenital heart disease guideline, published online 18 December 2025.** Supports adult congenital specialist care as patients transition from paediatric follow-up. [Joint guideline summary and publication](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2025-guideline-for-the-management-of-adults-with-congenital-heart-disease/top-things-to-know)

## Claims by scene

| Scene | Claim and animation check | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | The two circulation loops are parallel before the switch. The altered artery connections route blood through lungs and body in series. Keep both before tracers confined to their assigned loops. | A, B |
| P02 | The right ventricle leads to the aorta and the left to the pulmonary artery in d-TGA. Limited mixing can occur across communications. | A, B |
| P03 | Early specialist care, echo and possible temporary measures support the newborn and planning. Accurate coronary anatomy matters for transfer. | A, B |
| P04 | The former pulmonary root becomes the new aortic root. Its existing valve now serves the systemic outlet. Temporary cardiopulmonary bypass supports the operation. | A, B |
| P05 | Coronary openings move from the old aortic root to the new one with a rim of tissue. Follow flow from the new root into the same distal vessels. | A, B |
| P06 | The old aortic root becomes the pulmonary outlet. The pulmonary bifurcation can lie anterior to the new aorta in this repair. Keep vessel ends matched to their destinations. | A, B |
| P07 | The repaired flow, heart and valve function, breathing, circulation and feeding are assessed during hospital recovery. No uniform schedule is given. | A, B |
| P08 | Surveillance can look for coronary complications, pulmonary narrowing and neoaortic root or valve changes. Lifelong congenital heart care applies. | A–D |
| P09 | The switch changes the upper-vessel connections and coronary origins while keeping the ventricles in place. Trace the complete circulation and end on the sources. | A, B |
