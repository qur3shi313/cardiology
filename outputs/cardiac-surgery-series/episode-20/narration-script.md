# Arterial switch operation for d-transposition of the great arteries

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiac Surgery Education Series · Episode 20

English narration for ElevenLabs Alexander. Say d-TGA as “dee tee gee ay.” Record the paragraphs only.

## P01 · Two loops running in parallel

Watch the two markers. One goes around the body and comes back to the same side of the heart. The other travels to the lungs and back, without carrying enough of that oxygen to the body. That is the problem in d-transposition of the great arteries. An arterial switch changes the main artery connections so blood can pass through the lungs and then go to the body.

## P02 · Where each artery starts

Normally the right ventricle pumps into the pulmonary artery, which carries blood to the lungs. The left ventricle pumps into the aorta, which carries blood to the body. In d-transposition, the aorta starts at the right ventricle and the pulmonary artery starts at the left. Openings between parts of the circulation can let some blood mix, but the two main loops are still arranged in parallel.

## P03 · Diagnosis and early planning

This condition needs specialist care soon after birth. An ultrasound shows which ventricle each artery leaves and helps the team look for other differences in the heart. Some babies need temporary support to improve mixing before surgery. The care team also maps the small coronary arteries because they will need their own connection to the new aorta. The timing and plan depend on the baby's anatomy and condition.

## P04 · A new connection for the aorta

During the operation a heart-lung machine supports circulation. Look first at the artery going to the body. The surgeon reconnects its upper portion to the root above the left ventricle. That root was originally the base of the pulmonary artery. Its valve stays in that position and now works as the aortic valve. Blood leaving the left ventricle will pass through it into the aorta.

## P05 · Keeping the heart muscle supplied

The coronary arteries supply the heart muscle itself. Their openings were in the old aortic root, which will become part of the lung route. The surgeon transfers those openings, each with a small rim of tissue, to the new aortic root. The branches stay connected to the heart muscle. In the finished repair, they receive blood from the same aorta that supplies the body.

## P06 · Reconnecting the lung route

The surgeon reconnects the pulmonary arteries to the root above the right ventricle. That root was originally the base of the aorta. In the arrangement shown here, the pulmonary artery branches now cross in front of the aorta. With both connections complete, blood from the right ventricle can reach the lungs. Blood returning from the lungs can reach the left ventricle and then the body.

## P07 · Checking the result in hospital

The team watches how the heart pumps and checks the new routes. Heart imaging helps assess flow through the reconstructed arteries and how the valves work. In intensive care, staff monitor the baby's breathing and circulation. They also check how feeding progresses as the baby recovers. The care team decides when each support can come away and when the baby is ready to leave hospital.

## P08 · What follow-up looks for

Follow-up continues through childhood and into adult congenital heart care. The team checks for narrowing in the pulmonary arteries, changes in the new aortic root, leakage through its valve and problems with coronary blood flow. Some people need more tests or treatment as they grow. Ask your congenital heart team which parts of the repair they are watching and when you should return.

## P09 · From parallel loops to a connected route

Trace the repaired route once more. From the body to the right heart, through the lungs, into the left heart and out to the body. The coronary arteries now draw blood from the new aorta. That is the purpose of the arterial switch, and it brings our cardiac surgery series to a close.
