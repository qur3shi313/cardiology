## Episode 01A · Coronary angiography through the right wrist

### Facebook caption

What happens between the first needle at the wrist and the first image of a coronary artery?

In this episode, follow the instruments in order. A small wire enters through the needle and guides a short sheath into the radial artery. The sheath stays at the wrist. A separate guidewire helps the cardiologist position the diagnostic catheter at each coronary opening. Contrast through the catheter briefly outlines the vessels on X-ray, helping the team see where a vessel narrows.

The episode ends at the wrist, where staff use a compression band to control bleeding and monitor the site during recovery.

## Episode 01B · Coronary angiography through the right groin

### Facebook caption

How does a catheter reach the heart when the procedure starts at the groin?

This episode shows one approach to entering the right common femoral artery. A fine needle and wire provide access. A small introducer then lets the team exchange the wire and place a working sheath. The cardiologist guides a diagnostic catheter up through the aorta and injects contrast at each coronary opening to record X-ray images.

At the end of the test, the team may use direct pressure or a closure device to seal the opening in the artery. Staff check the groin site and tell the patient when it is safe to sit up and walk.
