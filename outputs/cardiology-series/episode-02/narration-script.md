## P01 · Angioplasty and stents

A coronary angiogram shows where a heart artery has narrowed. Coronary angioplasty can widen that narrowed passage. In this episode, we’ll follow one example from the first X-ray images to stent placement, the final check and recovery.

## P02 · Why the artery narrows

Coronary arteries supply the heart muscle with oxygen-rich blood. Plaque can build up in the artery wall and narrow the space where blood flows. In some heart attacks, a clot forms at a plaque and suddenly reduces or blocks blood flow.

## P03 · From diagnosis to treatment

During an angiogram, contrast outlines the artery on X-ray. The images show where the channel narrows and help the team decide what comes next. If treatment is appropriate, angioplasty can follow in the same session or be planned separately. The options may include medicines, angioplasty or bypass surgery.

## P04 · The guide catheter

Angioplasty is part of a treatment called percutaneous coronary intervention, or P C I. The team can reach the heart through a small sheath in an artery at the wrist or groin. In this example, a guide catheter reaches the opening of the coronary artery.

## P05 · Crossing the narrowing

Now a fine coronary guidewire passes through the guide catheter. The cardiologist watches on live X-ray as the wire crosses the narrowing. Once the tip is beyond it, the wire acts as a track for the balloon and stent delivery catheters.

## P06 · Preparing the narrowing

In this example, the team first prepares the narrowing with a separate balloon. The balloon arrives folded, slides over the guidewire and opens at the narrowed spot. As it inflates, it stretches the artery and presses the plaque toward the wall.

## P07 · Keeping the wire in place

The team deflates and removes that first balloon while the guidewire stays in place. This preparation step depends on the narrowing. In suitable cases, the team can advance the stent delivery system directly over the wire.

## P08 · How the stent arrives

Here comes the stent. It’s a tiny metal mesh tube, already mounted around a second, folded balloon. The balloon and stent move together over the guidewire. The cardiologist positions the assembly across the narrowed section before expanding it.

## P09 · Expanding the stent

When the delivery balloon inflates, it opens the mesh outward against the artery wall. The expanded stent supports the wider passage. The team then deflates the balloon and slides the balloon catheter back out. Watch the mesh stay in the artery.

## P10 · The medicine coating

Many coronary stents have a coating that gradually releases medicine into the area around the stent. These are called drug-eluting stents. The medicine helps lower the chance that tissue growth will narrow the treated area again.

## P11 · Checking the result

The team takes another set of X-ray images with contrast to check the treated segment and see how blood flows farther down the artery. In this example, the channel is wider. The team can also use imaging from inside the artery to examine how the stent has opened.

## P12 · Removing the equipment

After the team finishes its checks, it removes the wire and catheter. When the sheath is removed, a pressure band can help stop bleeding at the wrist. For a groin site, the team may use pressure or a closure device.

## P13 · Recovery and discharge

In recovery, the team checks your blood pressure, pulse and access site. Complications can include bleeding, artery injury or a clot. Your discharge plan explains wound care, activity, medicines and follow-up. The timing depends on how you are doing and whether the procedure was planned or followed a heart attack.

## P14 · Antiplatelet medicines

After a stent, your care team usually prescribes antiplatelet medicines. These make platelets less likely to clump together and help prevent a clot inside the stent. The choice and duration depend on your situation, including your risk of bleeding. Take them as prescribed and contact your cardiology team before stopping or changing them.

## P15 · Aftercare and urgent symptoms

Follow the activity and wound-care instructions from your hospital. Get emergency help for severe or persistent chest pain, bleeding that won’t stop, or a hand or foot that becomes cold or numb on the side used for the procedure. Ask your team about follow-up and cardiac rehabilitation.

## P16 · What treatment changes

The stent supports one treated section of the artery. Coronary artery disease still needs ongoing care. Ask your care team what was treated, which medicines to take and when your recovery plan allows you to return to usual activities.
