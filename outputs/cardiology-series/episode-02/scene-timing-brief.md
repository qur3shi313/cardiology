## Clinical continuity and review

- The main illustration follows a generic, single-vessel example with a focal plaque in the left anterior descending artery beyond the left main split. Show the left main continuing to the circumflex branch. Keep the target on the same branch, with the same plaque orientation and side-branch landmarks in the cutaway and X-ray views. The short wrist/groin recap refers viewers to the two angiography videos for full access sequences.
- Keep the vascular-access sheath at the wrist in the main example. Reuse the reviewed right wrist-to-aorta geometry from Episode 1A for the route overview. The guide catheter ends at the left coronary opening. The smaller coronary guidewire traverses the left main into the LAD and crosses the lesion inside the lumen. Hold its distal position through preparation balloon removal and stent-balloon placement and removal. Show the optional preparation balloon separately, then the stent crimped around its own delivery balloon from the first frame of that assembly.
- Show temporary flow reduction during balloon inflation and restoration of the channel after deflation. The mesh remains apposed to the wall. Keep visible plaque and realistic vessel geometry. The final angiogram is an illustrative immediate result and a check of downstream contrast flow. Internal imaging is a separate optional view that can assess stent expansion more directly.
- Closure and recovery vary by access site, clinical context and the patient’s care plan. Use the illustration to explain what the team does. Device sizing, inflation settings, medicine choices, dose, timing and individualized eligibility belong in clinician guidance.
- Check the episode’s assembled frame sequence, narration, source end card and caption timing before publication. The references were checked for the script. Obtain independent review of the actual clinical visuals if available before public release.

## Clinical sources and claim map

The letters in the timing table identify the supporting sources. Clinical education is based on the dated documents below.

- **A** Patient fact sheet. Explains wire crossing, balloon over the wire, plaque displacement, stent expansion and removal of delivery equipment. [American Heart Association, What Is Coronary Angioplasty?](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/Files/Health-Topics/Answers-by-Heart/What-Is-Coronary-Angioplasty.pdf)
- **B** Reviewed March 2026. Supports angiography and PCI decision, wrist/groin sheath, local access, X-ray guidance, stenting and optional internal imaging or pressure assessment. [Guy’s and St Thomas’ NHS Foundation Trust, Having a coronary angioplasty](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/coronary-angioplasty/having-coronary-angioplasty)
- **C** Updated 30 November 2023. Supports metal mesh and drug-eluting coating. See also [Getting a Stent](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/stents/during) for balloon deployment and removal. [NIH National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, What Are Stents? and Getting a Stent](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/stents)
- **D** Reviewed 4 October 2022. Listed review due date has passed. Used for established mechanical sequence and repeat contrast check. A [Royal Berkshire NHS patient leaflet](https://www.royalberkshire.nhs.uk/media/kexhv5qs/coming-in-for-angioplasty-and-stent_apr24.pdf) from April 2024 also describes an X-ray check of blood flow. The March 2026 Guy’s and St Thomas’ materials support the procedure and recovery. [NHS, Coronary angioplasty and stent insertion — How it’s performed](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-angioplasty/what-happens/)
- **E** Section 5.1 supports selected revascularization, medical therapy and physiology-informed decisions. The acute clot claim is covered by the 2025 acute coronary syndromes guideline in H. [2023 AHA/ACC multisociety guideline for chronic coronary disease](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001168)
- **F** Sections 10.2, 10.3 and 14.2 support drug-eluting stent selection, intracoronary imaging and ongoing antiplatelet therapy. Its revascularization decision framework supports presenting more than one treatment option. [2021 ACC/AHA/SCAI guideline for coronary artery revascularization](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001038)
- **G** Sections on arterial access, diagnostic assessment and initial PCI approach support the guide catheter, crossing and possible direct stenting. The STEMI-specific clinical recommendations are contextual evidence and are outside the illustrated example. [2024 SCAI expert consensus statement on patients with STEMI referred for primary PCI](https://www.jscai.org/article/S277293032401740X/pdf)
- **H** Sections 7.3 and 11.1 support intracoronary imaging in complex ACS PCI and tailoring antiplatelet therapy to bleeding and ischemic risk. ACS regimens and timing stay outside the narration. [2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI guideline for acute coronary syndromes](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)
- **I** Reviewed March 2026. Supports route-specific closure, site observation, recovery instructions, emergency symptoms and cardiac rehabilitation. [Guy’s and St Thomas’ NHS Foundation Trust, After a coronary angioplasty](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/coronary-angioplasty/after-coronary-angioplasty)

- **K** Updated 30 November 2023. Supports ongoing coronary disease care, clot risk with early medicine interruption and talking to the care team before changes. [NIH National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, Living With a Stent](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/stents/living-with)
- **L** Reviewed 4 October 2022. Listed review due date has passed. Used alongside I for severe chest pain, persistent access-site bleeding and cold or numb limb as urgent signs.
