## Anatomical and clinical continuity

- Label the patient’s right side. In the wrist example, follow the thumb-side radial artery into the brachial, axillary, right subclavian and brachiocephalic arteries, then the ascending aorta and aortic root. In the groin example, enter the common femoral artery above its split, follow the external and common iliac arteries into the abdominal and descending thoracic aorta, and round the arch to the root. The opposite side may be chosen in practice.
- Show the diagnostic catheter ending at a coronary opening above the aortic valve. Use the balloon or stent in the coronary branch only for the treatment example. Use the same orientation and site labels through the procedure and recovery. The existing wrist and groin angiogram films carry the complete device sequences.
- Give the acute coronary syndrome PCI finding its own on-screen context. Keep the stable PCI benefit scoped to access-site bleeding and blood-vessel complications. The planned angiogram beat describes routine practice when wrist access is suitable. Avoid transferring comparative outcomes between these settings.
- Show a small pressure band over the radial puncture. For the groin, use direct pressure or the alternate closure illustration. Keep any home-care timing, dressing plan, medication choices and activity limits specific to the patient’s discharge instructions. Keep contact and emergency cards distinct and location-neutral.
