# Device roles overview

The cutaway shows the right radial artery during catheter advancement. A short sheath stays at the wrist entry. The long guidewire moves through the sheath and along the artery. The hollow catheter advances through the sheath over that wire, whose tip remains farther along the artery. This is one moment in a representative catheterization. [American Heart Association, Cardiac Catheterization](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/diagnosing-a-heart-attack/cardiac-catheterization) · [SCAI transradial executive summary, Gaining Radial Artery Access](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/ccd.23052#page=4) · [SCAI transradial best practices update](https://www.scai.org/publications/clinical-documents/scai-expert-consensus-statement-update-best-practices-transradial)

The short access wire and dilator have already been removed in this scene. The cutaway keeps the fixed sheath visible and uses a translucent view to show the guidewire centered inside the catheter. [Terumo radial sheath instructions, steps 8–12](https://www.terumoindiaskilllab.com/assets/pdf/09_04_IFU_GSS.pdf)
