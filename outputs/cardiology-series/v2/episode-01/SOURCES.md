# Clinical source map

Coronary angiogram through the wrist or groin · Cardiology Education Series · Video 1

Source check on 11 November 2026. This cut uses the approved Alexander narration and the two previously reviewed procedure sequences. The two access views illustrate the patient's right side. Images of the coronary arteries and the groin access check are simulations.

| Scenes | Clinical content | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P01–P02 | A diagnostic coronary angiogram shows the channel within the arteries that supply the heart. Plaque can narrow it. Testing can be planned after evaluating symptoms and other results. In selected patients with suspected acute coronary syndromes, invasive assessment may be urgent and may lead to treatment. | [AHA coronary angiogram](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/diagnosing-a-heart-attack/coronary-angiogram) · [ESC chronic coronary syndromes guideline](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae177) · [2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI acute coronary syndromes guideline](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2024.11.009) |
| P03–P05 | A planned angiogram includes a review of medicines, kidney health and any prior contrast reaction. Follow local preparation instructions. The radial entry example is thumb-side, uses ultrasound, and uses local anaesthetic. | [Leeds Teaching Hospitals coronary angiography guide](https://www.leedsth.nhs.uk/patients/resources/understanding-coronary-angiography-and-coronary-intervention/) · [SCAI cath-lab best practices, §§4–6](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ccd.29744) · [SCAI transradial access consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31880380/) |
| P06–P12 | The needle comes out while the short radial wire remains. The sheath and tapered dilator go in over that wire. The short wire and dilator are removed before the longer diagnostic wire and catheter. The right radial route continues through the arm arteries to the aortic root under X-ray guidance. | [Radial introducer manufacturer instructions, English steps 1–14](https://www.terumoindiaskilllab.com/assets/pdf/09_04_IFU_GSS.pdf) · [AHA scientific statement on transradial angiography](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HCV.0000000000000035) · [NHS coronary angiogram leaflet](https://www.fhft.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-library/having-coronary-angiogram-fph) |
| P13–P23 | The groin example targets the common femoral artery above the division. The initial fine wire is checked in the main iliac artery. A temporary coaxial introducer allows exchange to a larger wire. The working sheath follows, then its dilator and exchange wire leave. The team may image the access site separately. The long diagnostic wire and catheter then use the iliac arteries and aorta to reach the root. | [SCAI safe femoral access technique](https://www.scai.org/quality-improvement-tools/qi-tips/safe-femoral-access-using-all-tools-toolbox) · [Micropuncture introducer manufacturer instructions, English steps 1–8](https://ifu.cookmedical.com/data/IFU_PDF/T_MPIS_REV2.PDF) · [SCAI safe femoral access tips](https://www.scai.org/quality-improvement-tools/qi-tips/safe-femoral-access-tips-tricks) · [Original selective transfemoral coronary arteriography study](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/89.5.815) |
| P24–P28 | The long diagnostic wire is withdrawn before each illustrated coronary contrast injection. A shallow catheter tip sits at the selected coronary opening above the valve. Left and right arteries are imaged separately. In the groin example a second diagnostic catheter is used through the same sheath. Equipment choice can vary. A series of X-rays records contrast passing through the selected arteries. | [Original selective coronary arteriography study](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/89.5.815) · [NHS angiography procedure](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/angiography/what-happens/) · [NHS coronary angiogram leaflet](https://www.fhft.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-library/having-coronary-angiogram-fph) · [2026 randomized diagnostic radial catheter comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42031154/) |
| P29–P30 | A narrowing can make the contrast-filled channel look thinner. Findings, symptoms and urgency inform next steps, which may include medicines, more testing, angioplasty or a discussion about bypass surgery. Selected patients can receive angioplasty in the same session. | [AHA coronary angiogram and treatment options](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/diagnosing-a-heart-attack/coronary-angiogram) · [Leeds Teaching Hospitals coronary angiography and intervention](https://www.leedsth.nhs.uk/patients/resources/understanding-coronary-angiography-and-coronary-intervention/) · [ACC/AHA/SCAI revascularization guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001038) |
| P31–P37 | At the end of the diagnostic example, the catheter leaves before the sheath. Staff use wrist compression or an appropriate groin method such as manual pressure or a closure device. They check the site and circulation. The need for supine observation and readiness for discharge vary by route and individual situation. | [SCAI cath-lab best practices, §6.3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ccd.29744) · [Guy's and St Thomas' NHS angiogram aftercare](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/coronary-angiogram/after-coronary-angiogram) · [Kingston and Richmond NHS day-case coronary angiogram](https://www.kingstonandrichmond.nhs.uk/patients-and-families/patient-leaflets/day-case-coronary-angiogram) |
| P38–P40 | Follow the care team's instructions for dressing, activity, medicines and follow-up. Mild soreness or a small bruise can occur. Increasing redness or pain, drainage or fever warrant contacting the care team. Heavy or persistent bleeding, rapidly growing swelling, a cold or numb hand or foot on the accessed side, new chest pressure or sudden shortness of breath need emergency care. Confirm the result and follow-up before leaving. | [Guy's and St Thomas' NHS angiogram aftercare](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/coronary-angiogram/after-coronary-angiogram) · [Interior Health coronary angiogram and angioplasty patient guide, p. 11](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/828243-coronary-angiogram-angioplsty-what-you-neeed-to-know-a-patient-family-guide.pdf) · [NHS angioplasty recovery](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-angioplasty/recovery/) · [AHA heart attack warning signs](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/warning-signs-of-a-heart-attack) |
