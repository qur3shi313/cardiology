# Clinical sources for wrist vs groin access

Source check on 11 November 2026. The comparison covers catheter-based coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). The two route drawings use the right side as an example.

| Scenes | Evidence used | Primary sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P01–P03 | A catheter can enter through a wrist or groin artery. The site is numbed, the catheter travels to the heart, and contrast outlines the coronary arteries on X-ray. PCI treats a narrowing and may use a balloon or stent. For invasive diagnostic angiography in the chronic coronary syndrome setting, the ESC describes radial access as standard when possible. | [AHA coronary angiogram](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/diagnosing-a-heart-attack/coronary-angiogram) · [Kingston and Richmond NHS day-case coronary angiogram](https://www.kingstonandrichmond.nhs.uk/patients-and-families/patient-leaflets/day-case-coronary-angiogram) · [NHS coronary angioplasty](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-angioplasty/what-happens/) · [2024 ESC chronic coronary syndromes guideline, §3.3.3](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae177) |
| P04 · Acute coronary syndrome | The 2025 US guideline prefers radial over femoral access in people with acute coronary syndromes undergoing PCI to reduce bleeding, vascular complications and death. The 2023 ESC guideline prefers radial access for invasive assessment in acute coronary syndromes, with or without PCI, and allows selected femoral access based on circulatory status and procedural factors. The narration assigns the US mortality finding to its stated acute coronary syndrome and PCI population. | [2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI acute coronary syndromes guideline, §7.1](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2024.11.009) · [2023 ESC acute coronary syndromes guideline, §9.1.1](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/44/38/3720/7243210) |
| P04 · Stable coronary disease | The 2021 US revascularization guideline recommends radial access in stable ischemic heart disease undergoing PCI to reduce access-site bleeding and vascular complications. | [2021 ACC/AHA/SCAI coronary revascularization guideline, §10.1](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001038) |
| P05–P06 | The team considers artery size and shape, equipment needs, previous bypass grafts and possible future use of a radial artery as a bypass graft. Femoral access may be chosen when radial access is impractical or when selected patients need large-bore mechanical circulatory support. Spasm or difficult anatomy can prompt a change of access. Left-sided access is an option when appropriate. | [2025 US acute coronary syndromes guideline, §7.1](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2024.11.009) · [SCAI primary PCI expert consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11624394/) · [SCAI radial spasm guidance](https://www.scai.org/quality-improvement-tools/qi-tips/catheter-guide-trick-sidestep-radial-artery-spasm-during-coronary) · [Northern Care Alliance NHS angiogram leaflet](https://www.northerncarealliance.nhs.uk/patient-information/patient-leaflets/cardiology-having-coronary-angiogram) |
| P02, P07–P09 | SCAI describes the common femoral target above the artery division and supports wrist compression bands, manual femoral pressure and suitable femoral closure devices. Recovery teams check the access site and circulation. Time to sit, walk or go home depends on access and closure, medicines, the procedure and the patient’s condition. Hospital discharge instructions address dressing care and return to activity. | [SCAI femoral access guidance](https://www.scai.org/quality-improvement-tools/qi-tips/safe-femoral-access-using-all-tools-toolbox) · [SCAI cath-lab best practices, §6.3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ccd.29744) · [Guy’s and St Thomas’ NHS angiogram recovery](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/coronary-angiogram/after-coronary-angiogram) · [NHS angioplasty recovery](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-angioplasty/recovery/) |
| P10–P11 | Mild bruising and tenderness can occur. Increasing pain, redness, swelling, drainage or fever need prompt contact. Heavy or persistent bleeding and a rapidly expanding lump can signal an emergency. Sudden coldness, pallor or numbness on the accessed side, severe chest pain and sudden breathing difficulty also warrant emergency help. | [Guy’s and St Thomas’ NHS angiogram recovery](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/coronary-angiogram/after-coronary-angiogram) · [NHS angioplasty recovery](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-angioplasty/recovery/) · [Interior Health angiogram and angioplasty patient guide, p. 11](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/828243-coronary-angiogram-angioplsty-what-you-neeed-to-know-a-patient-family-guide.pdf) · [AHA emergency signs](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/house-calls/when-to-call-911) |
