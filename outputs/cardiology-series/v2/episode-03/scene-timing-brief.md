## Anatomical and clinical continuity

- Follow the right radial artery at the thumb-side wrist into the upper-limb arteries and aortic root. The separate right common femoral example reaches the root through the iliac arteries and aorta. Use the earlier, checked illustrations and keep instrument movement inside the arterial channel. The coronary openings sit above the aortic valve.
- Maintain different appearances and labels for the local access wire and the longer wire that supports catheter travel. In the wrist example the sheath stays while the short wire and tapered insert come out. When the diagnostic catheter enters, the longer wire is already ahead. Both wires are gone from the artery before the illustrated coronary contrast run. Keep sheath and catheter lumens visibly distinct.
- Show the groin micropuncture as a selected variant. A temporary introducer and a larger exchange wire precede the working sheath in the earlier full animation. Use an explicit jump between the two selected views. Instrument shapes, catheter choice and route may vary. Give no dimensions, doses, injection settings, sterile technique or access-site coordinates.
- Keep the final position shallow at a coronary opening and show only the selected coronary tree filling. Separate the left and right runs and avoid implying that a diagnostic wire crosses a coronary narrowing. Reserve reading findings, comparing narrowing severity and treatment decisions for a later episode.
- Label the grayscale footage as a simulation. Use restrained clinical labels without product marks. Keep the “report symptoms” message distinct from brief warmth.
