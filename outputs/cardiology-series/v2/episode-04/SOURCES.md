# How clinicians read a coronary angiogram

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiology Education Series · v2 Video 4 · Approved topic 04

Checked on 11 November 2026. Letters match the visual scene table. Claims are paraphrased and educational. The graphics depict one illustrative angiogram and selected alternatives.

- **A** Supports contrast imaging of coronary artery narrowing and use of the result when discussing medicines, angioplasty or bypass. [American Heart Association — Coronary angiogram, reviewed February 2025](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/diagnosing-a-heart-attack/coronary-angiogram)
- **B** Supports anatomical stenosis characterization, limitations of visually estimated severity, and physiology for intermediate lesions. The script omits numerical thresholds and procedural details. [2021 ACC/AHA/SCAI guideline for coronary artery revascularization, sections 4.1 and 4.3](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001038)
- **C** Supports angiography, pressure assessment for uncertain functional significance, and further assessment for selected patients with persistent symptoms and nonobstructive arteries. [2024 ESC guideline for chronic coronary syndromes, sections 3.3.3 and 5.2](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/45/36/3415/7743115)
- **D** Supports the geometric effect of projecting a three-dimensional vessel segment onto a two-dimensional X-ray image. The angle animation is a generic demonstration. [Dumay et al., Determination of optimal angiographic viewing angles, primary evaluation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18218480/)
- **E** Supports the distinction between angiographic appearance and pressure-based physiological assessment in appropriate contexts. [American Heart Association scientific statement on invasive coronary physiological assessment](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001389)
- **F** Supports possible microvascular causes of symptoms despite apparently normal major coronary arteries and the role of further testing. The ESC guideline in C supports vasospasm as another possibility. [American Heart Association — Coronary microvascular disease](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/angina-chest-pain/coronary-microvascular-disease-mvd)
- **G** Supports the separate emergency context and clinical urgency of appropriate acute coronary syndrome management. [2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI guideline for acute coronary syndromes](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)
