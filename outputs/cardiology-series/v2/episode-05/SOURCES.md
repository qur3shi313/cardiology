# Recovery and next steps after a coronary angiogram

Elysian Biomedical LLP · Cardiology Education Series · v2 Video 5 · Approved topic 05

Checked on 11 November 2026. Letters match the visual scene table. The sources inform a general educational explanation and acknowledge variations in hospital practice and clinical circumstances.

- **A** Supports pressure cuff, manual pressure or selected groin closure devices, observations, recovery, discharge and wound concerns. The script leaves local thresholds and restrictions to the care team. [Guy’s and St Thomas’ NHS Foundation Trust — After a coronary angiogram, reviewed March 2026](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/coronary-angiogram/after-coronary-angiogram)
- **B** Supports observation of blood pressure, pulse and wound site after the test, and discussion of results before discharge. [North Tees and Hartlepool NHS Foundation Trust — Coronary angiogram](https://www.nth.nhs.uk/resources/coronary-angiogram/)
- **C** Supports access-site hemostasis and post-catheterization care. The visual shows generic pressure or a dressing and does not model an invasive closure technique. [SCAI expert consensus update on best practices in the cardiac catheterization laboratory, section 6.3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ccd.29744)
- **D** Supports recovery checks of blood pressure, heart rate, puncture site, limb perfusion and movement. Timing is case-dependent. [NSW Health — South Western Sydney Local Health District, Coronary angiography](https://www.swslhd.health.nsw.gov.au/Cardiovascular/coronary%20angiography.html)
- **E** Supports discussing findings and potential care pathways such as medicine, angioplasty or bypass. [American Heart Association — Coronary angiogram, reviewed February 2025](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/diagnosing-a-heart-attack/coronary-angiogram)
- **F** Supports further assessment when symptoms remain unexplained and selected physiology or additional testing is appropriate. [2024 ESC guideline for chronic coronary syndromes, sections 3.3.3 and 5.2](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/45/36/3415/7743115)
- **G** Supports procedure-specific differences in recovery, discharge medication, follow-up, antiplatelet therapy and cardiac rehabilitation after PCI. The listed review due date has passed. The episode uses established principles and current cath-specific corroboration. [NHS — Coronary angioplasty and stent insertion, Recovery](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-angioplasty/recovery/)
- **H** Supports checking with the care team before interrupting medicines after a stent. The script gives no medication regimen or duration. [NHLBI — Living With a Stent, updated November 2023](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/stents/living-with)
- **I** Supports bruising, soreness, infection cues and the importance of bleeding or a limb that feels cold or looks pale. [NHS — Angiography risks](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/angiography/risks/)
- **J** Supports rapid access-site swelling as an emergency warning. The script uses a location-neutral emergency action without locally specified steps. [Milton Keynes University Hospital NHS Foundation Trust — Discharge advice following coronary angiography, February 2025](https://www.mkuh.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/PILs-Discharge-advice-following-coronary-angiography-FEB25.pdf)
- **K** Supports chest discomfort and breathing difficulty as possible acute cardiac warning signs that require prompt emergency attention. [American Heart Association — Heart attack and stroke symptoms](https://www.heart.org/en/about-us/heart-attack-and-stroke-symptoms)
- **L** Corroborates serious chest pain, persistent access-site bleeding and sudden discoloration, coldness or numbness of the accessed limb after catheter-based coronary care. [NHS — Coronary angioplasty and stent insertion, Recovery, emergency symptoms section](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-angioplasty/recovery/)
