# Coronary drug-coated balloons: clinical sources

This film uses a generic coronary in-stent restenosis example. The device illustrations are original. References to regulatory decisions are limited to their named jurisdiction and device. The film makes no claim about availability, local approval, suitability for a particular patient or an Elysian Biomedical product.

- P01–P03: [SCAI expert consensus on in-stent restenosis and stent thrombosis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11308135/) (2023). Supports distinct mechanisms, imaging, why a repeat narrowing is evaluated and treatment decisions.
- P04–P07 and P09: The [FDA approval record](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpma/pma.cfm?ID=P230035) dates the named US in-stent restenosis approval to 29 February 2024. The [FDA summary of safety and effectiveness](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/P230035B.pdf) supports preparation, delivery, local transfer, full deflation and removal. The film illustrates the concept using generic tools and gives no device settings. The original stent stays in place after the balloon is removed.
- P07: The [BASKET-SMALL 2 randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30170854/) studied drug-coated balloon treatment for selected de novo lesions in small coronary arteries. This is the research example in the narration. The FDA record supports the distinct historical US regulatory statement for in-stent restenosis.
- P08: [NHS coronary angioplasty recovery](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/coronary-angioplasty/recovery/) supports general post-PCI recovery, prescribed medicines and emergency symptoms. The patient follows the treating team’s plan.
