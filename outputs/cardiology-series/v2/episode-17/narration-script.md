## P01 · Planning starts before the procedure

How does the team know if a new valve will fit? Before a catheter-based valve replacement, the Heart Team builds a map of the heart valve and the vessels that could carry the device to it.

## P02 · Checking how serious the narrowing is

First, the team reviews the person’s symptoms and the severity of aortic stenosis. An echocardiogram shows the valve opening and how blood moves through it. It also helps assess the pumping chamber. The team uses these findings to decide whether valve replacement needs to be considered.

## P03 · Measuring where the new valve would sit

A CT scan can show the aortic root in detail. The team measures the ring where the new valve would sit and studies nearby structures, including the openings of the coronary arteries. It also checks where calcium is concentrated. Those details matter for how a replacement valve could fit.

## P04 · Assessing the delivery route

The same type of scan can show a potential route through the arteries. The most common catheter route is through an artery in the groin. The team checks the route’s shape and size and where there is calcium. If that route is unsuitable, the team discusses other options.

## P05 · Other health checks

The assessment can also include blood tests, a heart rhythm tracing and a look at the coronary arteries. The team considers other health conditions, day-to-day function and what the patient wants from treatment. These details help it weigh the potential benefits and risks of the available options.

## P06 · How a recommendation is made

The Heart Team brings together specialists in imaging, catheter treatment and surgery. They review the test results and discuss which options are suitable. Sometimes the assessment leads to further testing or a surgical opinion. The recommendation takes both the valve anatomy and the patient’s wider health into account.

## P07 · Preparing for the discussion

The patient can ask why an option is being recommended, what its risks are, and what recovery could involve. The team explains the proposed next steps.
