## P01 · What happens after the valve is in place?

The new valve is working. What happens next? The care team watches the heart rhythm, checks the site where the catheter went in, and uses an ultrasound scan to assess the new valve. These early checks guide the next part of recovery.

## P02 · The first checks in hospital

In this example, the catheter reached the heart through an artery in the groin. The team checks the small covered wound for bleeding or swelling. It also monitors heart rhythm and blood pressure. How long the patient stays depends on recovery, the access route, and whether the team finds any problems.

## P03 · Why the team watches the rhythm

The new valve sits close to part of the heart’s electrical pathway. Some people develop a change in the way electrical signals travel after TAVI. If that happens, the team may monitor them for longer. Some people need a permanent pacemaker to help maintain a reliable rhythm.

## P04 · Checking the valve on ultrasound

An echocardiogram uses ultrasound to assess how the replacement valve opens and closes and how blood moves through it. The team can also check whether any blood leaks around the edge of the valve. These findings, together with the other checks, inform the recovery plan.

## P05 · The home recovery plan

Before the patient goes home, the team explains how to care for the wound, build up activity, and follow the prescribed medicine plan. It also explains whom to contact with concerns and when the next check is due. The details can vary with other conditions and how the procedure went.

## P06 · Knowing when to get urgent help

The discharge instructions explain which warning signs need urgent attention. Severe bleeding from the access site or possible signs of stroke need emergency help. Stroke signs can include a drooping face, sudden arm weakness, or speech difficulty. Call local emergency services immediately if these occur.

## P07 · Follow-up checks

Follow-up helps the team see how the patient feels and how the valve is working over time. The team may review symptoms, medicines and rhythm, and arrange a repeat ultrasound. Before the patient leaves hospital, it is useful to know who will arrange follow-up and whom to call with questions.
